17/08/2026: About NaturalMotion's endorphin™

History:

endorphin was a "computer software for simulating the physical movement of human beings and other animate objects", it was unique in a sense that they trained neural networks to come up with behaviours that could be applied to a simulated ragdoll. The main motivation behind endorphin was the research paper "Biologically Inspired Control Of Physically Simulated Bipeds", written by Torsten Reil, which would give start to NaturalMotion & endorphin.

endorphin was often accompanied by the fancy term "Virtual Motion Capture", most notable in the 1.1 version of endorphin.

A notable past use of endorphin was in a pepsi ad work by The Mill, called "Pepsi Max - Glue Boy". this video goes in detail about the behind the scenes of the ad. it was also seemingly used in the movie Mad Max: Fury Road (see this article)

It is thought that, due to the absurd and varying price put on endorphin by NaturalMotion (around 1 thousand dollars upwards to 13 thousand dollars), that the company received little to no requests of purchase or renting about endorphin, and with the growth of their other products (euphoria & morpheme), endorphin was discontinued in 2014, with the official community forum also being shut down.

The discontinuation of endorphin did not mean it was completely discarded. The 2023 GTA V leak revealed multiple builds of endorphin version 3.0 being repurposed by rockstar games studios to make physical character rigs. a snippet of a document found in the leak further confirms this:

"endorphin lets you create your own character rigs using a powerful editing environment. A range of productivity tools such as mirroring and alignment make it easy to build character rigs that you can use with both kinematics and dynamics. A range of editors let you easily visualise and manage complex scenes composed of many characters. With endorphin Python and Lua scripting many recurring editing tasks can be automated. Characters can be animated or simulated directly in endorphin (using Nvidia's PhysX engine), or can be exported for use in morpheme or euphoria."

The earliest build of 3.0 is found to be in march 2010 and the latest is november 2011. Funnily enough, all of the 3.0 endorphin builds come with a readme text file containing nothing but the phrase "This product does nothing."

In the USA, the endorphin trademark has had its registration cancelled since 2018(serial number 78786313), and is expired in the UK since april of 2026 (trademark number UK00002419276).

The only surviving copies of endorphin that prevail to this day (and that i know of) are the following:

* endorphin 1.1 (internal version 1.01);
* endorphin 2.5.2 Professinal Edition (internal version 2.5.2.5052);
* endorphin 2.5.3 Learning Edition (internal version 2.5.3.5575);
* endorphin 2.7.1 Learning Ediiton (internal version 2.7.1.7332).

Technical specifications:

all endorphin versions use the MathEngine Karma physics engine (version 1.3.0 build 631), for rendering it uses the OpenGL API.
Nearly all of endorphin assets are XML files compressed and encrypted based on if its for the professional or learning edition (see my other blog "How Naturalmotion endorphin assets are encrypted").
It is built upon the Visual Studio MFC template.
They've also lazily copied a splash screen code example from a codeproject post in 2002, from author John O'Byrne, for use in their thousand dollar project.
Retail/professional versions of endorphin are protected by hasp technology, with the main executable being encrypted/packed by hasp envelope. this means that, without access to the dongle that is accompanied by the product, it is basically impossible to use or decrypt the executable. this is true for almost all of naturalmotion products.
